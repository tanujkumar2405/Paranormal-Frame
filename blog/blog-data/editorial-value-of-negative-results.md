# The Value of Publishing Negative Results in Anomalous Research

An investigation that produces no unusual reading may appear uninteresting. In fact, a well-designed session with no detected event can be valuable. It tells readers what was tested, under what conditions and what the instruments did not detect.

**Publication bias.** Dramatic positive findings are more likely to be shared than ordinary or null results. Over time, this can create a distorted impression that unusual events are common or that a method works more reliably than it does. Publishing negative results helps correct that imbalance.

**What a negative result means.** It does not prove that an event can never occur. It means the defined test did not detect the specified outcome under the recorded conditions and within the method's sensitivity. The wording matters. “No event occurred” may be too strong; “no event meeting the pre-defined criterion was detected” is more precise.

**Report the method.** Include session duration, equipment, calibration, event definitions, missing data, environmental conditions and analysis rules. A negative result without method details is difficult to interpret. A positive result should be reported with the same transparency.

**Learning from null findings.** A sensor may have a high false-trigger rate. A room may be acoustically quieter than expected. A witness report may not recur under controlled conditions. Each result can improve future study design.

**AERM editorial standard.** We should not select only the most dramatic clips from a session. If a report includes excerpts, explain how they were selected and make the broader context available where privacy and safety permit. Corrections and follow-up tests should be linked to the original report.

**Conclusion.** Negative results are not failed investigations. They are part of the evidence record. A research culture that publishes only excitement cannot reliably learn from its own work.

## AERM Research and Reporting Checklist

Before publication, confirm that the report:
- States the question and the observation separately from any interpretation.
- Identifies the location and conditions at a level that respects privacy and safety.
- Records equipment, settings, placement and known limitations.
- Preserves original files and documents any processing.
- Describes controls, alternative explanations and unsuccessful tests.
- Reports uncertainty and avoids claims stronger than the evidence.
- Has permission for all identifiable recordings, quotations and images.

> **AERM Core Principle:** No anomaly is considered paranormal until environmental, psychological, technical, and scientific explanations have been systematically evaluated.
