# Informed Consent in Paranormal Field Research: Respect, Privacy and the Right to Withdraw

An investigation can involve private property, personal experiences, recordings and emotionally charged stories. Ethical research requires more than obtaining access to a location. Participants should understand what the team intends to do, what information will be collected and how it may be used.

**What informed consent means.** Consent should be voluntary, informed and specific. Explain the purpose of the activity, expected duration, recording methods, foreseeable risks, data storage, publication plans and contact route for questions. Avoid promising a discovery or implying that participation will validate a witness's interpretation.

**Right to withdraw.** Participants should be able to stop their participation without pressure. Explain what happens to data already collected and any limits on withdrawal after material has been anonymised, published or incorporated into analysis. Follow applicable law and institutional requirements.

**Privacy and confidentiality.** Audio, video, names, faces, addresses and personal accounts can identify people. Collect only what is necessary. Use pseudonyms where appropriate, restrict access and obtain separate permission for public release of identifiable material.

**Vulnerable participants.** Take particular care where people are distressed, young, dependent on others or unable to provide informed consent. Do not use fear, staged events or deceptive claims to recruit participants. Seek appropriate guardian permission and professional ethical guidance where required.

**AERM consent checklist.** Explain the project in plain language; describe recording; clarify publication options; state risks and limits; confirm voluntary participation; document consent; provide a copy or contact details; and respect withdrawal requests according to the agreed policy.

**Avoiding coercion.** Do not tell a witness that they must participate to receive help, closure or validation. Do not exploit grief or fear for dramatic content. Compensation, if offered, should not be so large that it undermines voluntary choice.

**Conclusion.** Ethical practice protects participants and strengthens research credibility. A compelling story never overrides a person's right to understand, choose and set boundaries.

## AERM Research and Reporting Checklist

Before publication, confirm that the report:
- States the question and the observation separately from any interpretation.
- Identifies the location and conditions at a level that respects privacy and safety.
- Records equipment, settings, placement and known limitations.
- Preserves original files and documents any processing.
- Describes controls, alternative explanations and unsuccessful tests.
- Reports uncertainty and avoids claims stronger than the evidence.
- Has permission for all identifiable recordings, quotations and images.

> **AERM Core Principle:** No anomaly is considered paranormal until environmental, psychological, technical, and scientific explanations have been systematically evaluated.
