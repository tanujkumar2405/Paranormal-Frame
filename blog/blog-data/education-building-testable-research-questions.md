# How to Build a Research Question from a Paranormal Claim

A statement such as “this room is haunted” is too broad to test directly. It combines a location, an interpretation and an assumed cause. Research begins when the claim is translated into a question that can be answered through observation or measurement.

A useful research question identifies a phenomenon, a setting, a measurable outcome and a time frame. For example: “Does the corridor show a higher rate of unexplained acoustic transients during unoccupied periods than during matched control periods?” This question does not assume what caused the sounds. It defines what will be counted and compared.

A hypothesis predicts an observable result. A null hypothesis typically states that there is no difference or association under the chosen model. The alternative hypothesis states what difference or relationship is expected. Hypotheses should be specific enough that evidence could count against them. If every possible outcome is interpreted as support, the claim is not meaningfully testable.

**Operational definitions.** Define what counts as an event before collecting data. For an acoustic transient, specify amplitude threshold, duration, frequency range and exclusions such as known door or footstep sounds. Define “unoccupied” and state how occupancy is verified. Use the same rule throughout the study.

**Variables and controls.** Identify the independent variable, outcome measure and potential confounders. If comparing two rooms, differences in microphone, gain, occupancy, temperature and background activity may influence the result. Controls help isolate the factor under study; they do not guarantee perfect certainty.

**AERM learning exercise.** Choose one claim from a witness account. Rewrite it as a neutral observation. Convert that observation into one measurable research question. State a prediction, a result that would weaken the prediction, and the data required. Ask another person to review the wording for assumptions or leading language before fieldwork.

**Common errors.** Avoid changing event definitions after seeing results, collecting only dramatic examples, treating anecdotes as rates, or using a device's marketing label as a scientific measurement. Pilot testing can reveal whether a method is practical, but pilot data should not silently become confirmatory evidence.

**Conclusion.** Good research questions narrow a story into something observable. This makes an investigation more rigorous and more respectful: witnesses can be heard without accepting the interpretation in advance.

## AERM Research and Reporting Checklist

Before publication, confirm that the report:
- States the question and the observation separately from any interpretation.
- Identifies the location and conditions at a level that respects privacy and safety.
- Records equipment, settings, placement and known limitations.
- Preserves original files and documents any processing.
- Describes controls, alternative explanations and unsuccessful tests.
- Reports uncertainty and avoids claims stronger than the evidence.
- Has permission for all identifiable recordings, quotations and images.

> **AERM Core Principle:** No anomaly is considered paranormal until environmental, psychological, technical, and scientific explanations have been systematically evaluated.
