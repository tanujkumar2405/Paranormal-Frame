# How to Review an EMF Meter Without Falling for Its Marketing

An EMF meter review should answer a simple question: what physical quantity does this instrument measure, over what range and with what uncertainty? Labels such as “ghost detector” or “spirit sensor” are marketing language, not technical specifications.

**First identify the measurement.** Some instruments measure low-frequency magnetic fields; others detect electric fields or radio-frequency energy. A combined consumer device may use several sensors with different ranges. These are not interchangeable. A reading in milligauss, microtesla, volts per metre or milliwatts per square metre describes different quantities.

**Review criteria.** Record frequency response, measurement range, resolution, accuracy specification, sampling or update rate, sensor orientation, alarm behaviour, calibration options and environmental operating limits. Check whether the device provides raw numerical data or only coloured lights and sound alerts.

**Bench evaluation.** Compare the meter with a suitable reference instrument in a controlled environment. Test repeatability at fixed positions and orientations. Introduce known sources only where safe and within the instrument's rating. Record how the device responds to distance and orientation. Do not expose equipment or people to hazardous electrical conditions.

**Field usability.** Assess battery life, display readability, logging capability, build quality, manual clarity and whether the alarm can be disabled. Note whether the meter is easily triggered by phones, chargers, lighting drivers or nearby transmitters.

**A review score should not hide uncertainty.** A device can be well-built yet unsuitable for a specific measurement. State what was tested, what was not tested and whether the review sample was supplied by a manufacturer. Avoid implying that a high reading indicates a paranormal presence.

**Suggested review format.** Product identity; intended measurement; manufacturer specifications; test setup; repeatability results; known limitations; safety considerations; value for the stated research use; final suitability rating. Keep “suitability for environmental measurement” separate from “ability to detect paranormal activity”—the latter has no validated consumer-meter benchmark.

**Conclusion.** A credible equipment review evaluates the instrument as a measuring device. It does not endorse the supernatural claims used to sell it.
