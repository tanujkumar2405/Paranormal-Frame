# The Influence of Carbon Monoxide Exposure on Reported Paranormal Experiences: An Environmental Health Perspective

> **Not every unexplained experience has an extraordinary cause. Sometimes, the greatest mystery is an unseen environmental hazard.**

Throughout history, reports of apparitions, unexplained voices, shadow figures, feelings of being watched, and overwhelming sensations of fear have often been associated with supposedly haunted locations. While such experiences deserve to be documented and investigated respectfully, responsible investigators must also consider whether the surrounding environment may have contributed to what was experienced.

One environmental factor that has received considerable scientific attention is **carbon monoxide (CO)**.

Carbon monoxide is an invisible, odorless, and highly toxic gas capable of affecting the human nervous system. Exposure can impair cognitive function, alter perception, and produce neurological symptoms that may be interpreted as unusual or unexplained experiences.

At **Anomalous Evidence Research & Media (AERM)**, environmental health assessments are an integral part of evidence-based investigations because understanding the environment is essential before evaluating extraordinary claims.

---

# Executive Summary

Carbon monoxide exposure may:

- Reduce oxygen delivery to the brain.
- Impair judgment and concentration.
- Produce headaches, dizziness, and confusion.
- Contribute to visual or auditory disturbances.
- Increase anxiety and feelings of unease.
- Create experiences that may appear unexplained.

This does **not** imply that all reported paranormal experiences are caused by carbon monoxide. Rather, it highlights the importance of environmental testing as part of a comprehensive investigative process.

---

# What Is Carbon Monoxide?

Carbon monoxide (CO) is a toxic gas produced whenever carbon-based fuels burn incompletely.

Unlike smoke, carbon monoxide:

- Has no color.
- Has no odor.
- Has no taste.
- Cannot be detected by human senses.

Because of these characteristics, people may unknowingly be exposed for extended periods.

---

# Common Sources of Carbon Monoxide

Potential sources include:

- Gas furnaces
- Fireplaces
- Wood-burning stoves
- Gas water heaters
- Portable generators
- Vehicle exhaust
- Charcoal grills
- Kerosene heaters
- Gas-powered equipment

Faulty appliances, blocked chimneys, poor ventilation, or improper fuel combustion can increase indoor carbon monoxide levels.

---

# How Carbon Monoxide Affects the Human Body

Carbon monoxide enters the bloodstream through the lungs and binds to hemoglobin approximately **200–250 times more readily than oxygen**. This reduces the blood's ability to transport oxygen to vital organs, particularly the brain and heart.

As oxygen delivery decreases, neurological and physiological symptoms begin to develop.

Possible symptoms include:

- Headaches
- Dizziness
- Fatigue
- Difficulty concentrating
- Confusion
- Blurred vision
- Ringing in the ears
- Nausea
- Shortness of breath
- Anxiety
- Hallucinations
- Loss of consciousness

The severity of symptoms depends on both concentration and duration of exposure.

# Why This Matters During Investigations

Many reports received by investigators involve experiences such as:

- Seeing shadow-like figures.
- Hearing unexplained voices.
- Feeling an unseen presence.
- Sudden fear or panic.
- Sleep disturbances.
- Confusion or memory lapses.
- Visual distortions.
- A sensation of pressure in the room.

These experiences are genuine to those who report them.

However, similar symptoms have also been documented in cases of carbon monoxide exposure.

Recognizing this possibility allows investigators to evaluate the environment objectively before considering less conventional explanations.

# A Historical Lesson

One of the most widely discussed examples in environmental health involves occupants of a home who believed they were experiencing paranormal activity.

The family reported:

- Apparitions
- Strange sounds
- Oppressive feelings
- Visual disturbances
- Persistent headaches

An inspection later revealed a malfunctioning heating system leaking carbon monoxide into the home.

After the source was repaired, the reported experiences stopped.

While this case does **not** demonstrate that all unusual reports share the same explanation, it serves as a reminder that environmental hazards should never be overlooked during investigations.

---

# Carbon Monoxide Is Only One Environmental Variable

Professional investigators should avoid replacing one assumption with another.

Carbon monoxide is only one of many environmental factors capable of influencing perception.

Other important variables include:

- Elevated carbon dioxide (CO₂)
- Poor ventilation
- Mold contamination
- Volatile Organic Compounds (VOCs)
- Infrasound
- Poor indoor lighting
- Extreme temperatures
- Humidity
- Psychological stress
- Sleep deprivation

Each factor should be evaluated individually through appropriate environmental assessment.

---

# The Role of Environmental Health Assessments

Environmental health assessments help investigators understand whether environmental conditions may have influenced reported experiences.

An assessment may include:

- Carbon monoxide monitoring
- Carbon dioxide measurements
- Oxygen concentration
- Temperature
- Relative humidity
- Airflow assessment
- Indoor air quality testing
- Building inspection
- Ventilation evaluation

These measurements provide valuable context for interpreting witness reports.

---

# Public Safety Comes First

Carbon monoxide is a serious public health hazard.

If investigators suspect elevated carbon monoxide levels during an investigation, they should prioritize occupant safety above continuing evidence collection.

Recommended actions include:

- Leave the affected area immediately.
- Move to fresh air.
- Contact emergency services if exposure is suspected.
- Seek medical attention for anyone experiencing symptoms.
- Have heating systems and fuel-burning appliances inspected by qualified professionals.
- Do not re-enter the building until it has been declared safe.

Protecting lives is always more important than completing an investigation.

---

# What Investigators Should Learn

Carbon monoxide teaches an important lesson about evidence-based investigation.

Investigators should never assume that an unusual experience has an extraordinary explanation simply because it feels extraordinary.

Likewise, investigators should avoid assuming that one environmental factor explains every report.

Professional investigation requires:

- Careful observation.
- Environmental testing.
- Objective documentation.
- Critical evaluation.
- Transparent reporting.

Evidence—not assumptions—should guide every conclusion.

# Conclusion

Carbon monoxide exposure illustrates why environmental health should never be overlooked during investigations of reported paranormal experiences.

Its effects on human physiology demonstrate that environmental conditions can sometimes influence perception in ways that feel deeply unusual and entirely real to those experiencing them.

This does not diminish the experiences reported by witnesses.

Instead, it reinforces the importance of investigating responsibly, collecting objective data, and evaluating every case with an open but critical mind.

At AERM, we believe that understanding the environment is one of the most important steps toward understanding the experience itself.

> **Responsible investigation begins by protecting people, understanding the environment, and allowing evidence—not expectation—to shape the conclusion.**
