# Radio Frequency Interference: Can Wireless Signals Create Misleading Paranormal Evidence?
## Introduction

A recorder produces a faint voice-like sound. An EMF meter changes its reading as an investigator approaches a wall. A wireless camera briefly loses its connection. These events may feel connected, particularly in a location already associated with unusual reports. Yet each can have ordinary electronic explanations that deserve careful testing.

Radio-frequency (RF) energy is used by broadcast radio, mobile networks, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth and many other systems. Electronic devices are designed to operate in environments containing electromagnetic energy, but imperfect shielding, wiring, grounding, connectors or device design can allow unwanted interference to affect measurements.

The central research question is not whether radio waves are mysterious. It is whether a particular signal could have entered, influenced or been misinterpreted by the equipment used during an investigation.

## 1. What Is Radio-Frequency Interference?

Radio frequency refers broadly to electromagnetic signals used for wireless communication. RF interference occurs when unwanted electromagnetic energy disrupts the intended operation of a receiver or electronic system.

Potential sources include:
- Nearby mobile phones transmitting or receiving data.
- Wi-Fi access points and Bluetooth devices.
- Two-way radios and wireless microphones.
- Broadcast transmitters and other radio services.
- Poorly shielded power supplies, cables or electronic equipment.

Interference can be conducted through cables or power connections, or coupled into a device through its enclosure, antenna or circuit paths. The exact mechanism depends on the equipment and signal.

## 2. How Interference Can Affect Investigation Equipment

**Audio recorders and microphones:** An inadequately shielded audio chain may pick up RF energy that is converted into audible interference by nonlinear components or poor connections. Digital devices may instead experience dropouts, glitches or other faults. A sound resembling a voice should therefore be checked against the recording system and nearby transmitters.

**Wireless cameras:** Wi-Fi congestion, weak signal strength, competing transmitters or poor network configuration may cause delayed, missing or corrupted video. A gap in a wireless stream is not evidence that an object disappeared.

**EMF meters:** Different meters measure different quantities and frequency ranges. A simple low-frequency magnetic-field meter is not a universal detector of all electromagnetic radiation. Nearby electrical wiring, motors, transformers and transmitters may produce readings depending on the instrument's design and bandwidth.

**Cables and sensors:** Long or poorly routed cables can act as unintended antennas. Loose connectors, ground loops and power-supply noise may also create unstable readings.

## 3. RF Energy Is Not the Same as an EMF Reading

"Electromagnetic field" is a broad term. Static magnetic fields, low-frequency fields around electrical wiring and radio-frequency fields are not interchangeable. A device designed to measure one range may not detect another reliably.

Before interpreting a reading, investigators should identify:
- The instrument's model and stated measurement range.
- Whether it measures electric field, magnetic field, or another quantity.
- Units, calibration status and limitations.
- Sensor orientation and distance from the source.
- Whether the reading is an average, peak or instantaneous value.

Without these details, a number on a display has limited scientific meaning.

## 4. A Controlled RF Interference Check

A practical test should be conducted in a safe, controlled environment—not by transmitting on restricted frequencies or disrupting communications.

1. Record a baseline with the equipment operating in its normal configuration.
2. Document nearby wireless devices and known transmitters.
3. Keep recorder position, gain, cables and power source unchanged.
4. Compare recordings with wireless devices in ordinary operating states, where doing so is safe and permitted.
5. Repeat measurements and record timestamps.
6. Where available, use suitable RF measurement equipment operated by someone competent to interpret it.
7. Preserve original files and note every change to the setup.

Do not deliberately jam signals, interfere with public communications or transmit on frequencies without authorisation. A controlled investigation does not require unlawful interference.

## 5. Distinguishing a Signal from an Interpretation

Suppose an audio file contains a short burst of clicking and a low sound. The first report calls it a whisper. A defensible review would preserve the original recording, inspect its waveform and spectrogram, listen at normal speed, check microphone and cable arrangements, and document nearby electronic devices.

If the sound changes when a particular device is operating, that is a useful lead. It is not enough by itself to establish causation: the test should be repeated while controlling other variables.

## 6. AERM Field Documentation Checklist

For every relevant event, record:
- Device make, model, serial number and settings.
- Microphone, sensor and cable locations.
- Nearby wireless devices and their approximate positions.
- Time, duration and environmental conditions.
- Baseline readings before and after the event.
- Any equipment movement, reboot or connection loss.
- Original file names and integrity hashes, where practical.
- Alternative explanations considered and tests performed.

## Conclusion

RF interference is a testable technical possibility, not a universal explanation for unusual recordings. Wireless transmissions, electrical noise, cable faults and equipment limitations can affect observations in different ways. Investigators should identify what each instrument actually measures, preserve original data and conduct repeatable checks before assigning meaning to an alert or sound.

For AERM, the responsible conclusion may be that an event was caused by interference, that it remains unresolved, or that the available data is insufficient. An unexplained signal is not automatically a paranormal one.
