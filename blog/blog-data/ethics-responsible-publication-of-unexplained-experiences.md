# Publishing Unexplained Experiences Responsibly: Avoiding Sensationalism and Harm

A research article, documentary or social-media post can shape how audiences understand a witness, a location and an unexplained event. Sensational editing may attract attention, but it can also misrepresent uncertainty, damage reputations and encourage unsafe behaviour.

**Separate evidence from narrative.** Label direct observations, witness recollections, instrument readings and interpretations separately. Do not present a dramatic reconstruction as original footage. If a clip has been shortened, enhanced or colour-graded, disclose that fact.

**Avoid certainty inflation.** Words such as “proof,” “confirmed” and “undeniable” should be reserved for conclusions that the evidence actually supports. “Unexplained in this review” is more accurate than “paranormal confirmed.” A lack of an immediate explanation is not proof of an extraordinary cause.

**Protect people and places.** Remove identifying details when publication could expose a witness to harassment or reveal a vulnerable site. Do not publish access routes to dangerous abandoned buildings. Obtain permission for images, recordings and quotations.

**Context matters.** Include ordinary explanations tested, limitations, failed tests and relevant counterevidence. If a witness disputes the account, document the disagreement fairly. Avoid thumbnails or headlines that imply a conclusion not supported by the article.

**Editorial checklist.** Verify dates and sources; preserve original media; distinguish reconstruction from evidence; obtain consent; check factual claims; include uncertainty; avoid defamatory speculation; and provide a correction route.

**Conclusion.** Responsible communication does not make a story dull. It gives the audience enough information to judge the evidence independently. Credibility is built by showing what is known, what is uncertain and what remains to be tested.

## AERM Research and Reporting Checklist

Before publication, confirm that the report:
- States the question and the observation separately from any interpretation.
- Identifies the location and conditions at a level that respects privacy and safety.
- Records equipment, settings, placement and known limitations.
- Preserves original files and documents any processing.
- Describes controls, alternative explanations and unsuccessful tests.
- Reports uncertainty and avoids claims stronger than the evidence.
- Has permission for all identifiable recordings, quotations and images.

> **AERM Core Principle:** No anomaly is considered paranormal until environmental, psychological, technical, and scientific explanations have been systematically evaluated.
