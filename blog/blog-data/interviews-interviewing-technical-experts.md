# Interviewing Technical Experts: How to Ask Useful Questions About Instruments and Buildings

Technical experts can help investigators understand building systems, instruments and environmental conditions. But an expert interview is useful only when the questions are precise and the answers are represented accurately.

**Choose the right expertise.** Match the question to the discipline. An electrician can assess electrical installation concerns; an acoustician can examine sound propagation; a structural engineer can assess building stability. Do not ask one professional to certify matters outside their competence.

**Prepare the evidence.** Provide a concise description of the observation, relevant measurements, device specifications and site conditions. Avoid presenting a preferred paranormal interpretation as established fact. Ask the expert what additional information is needed.

**Ask testable questions.** “What could produce a 50 Hz component in this recording?” is more useful than “Is this energy paranormal?” Ask about likely mechanisms, measurement limits, safety implications and what test would distinguish competing explanations.

**Clarify certainty.** Ask whether the answer is a general possibility, a likely explanation or a conclusion supported by site-specific inspection. Record assumptions and missing data. Do not turn “could be consistent with” into “was caused by.”

**Consent and attribution.** Obtain permission to record and quote. Confirm professional title and affiliation. Offer the expert a chance to check technical quotations for accuracy without granting control over the editorial conclusion.

**Interview structure.** Explain the project; state the specific question; describe the measurement; ask about mechanisms; explore limitations; ask what evidence would change the assessment; and summarise the answer back for confirmation.

**Conclusion.** Expert interviews should sharpen the investigation, not serve as authority theatre. The strongest contribution is a clear account of what can be concluded, what cannot, and what should be measured next.

## AERM Research and Reporting Checklist

Before publication, confirm that the report:
- States the question and the observation separately from any interpretation.
- Identifies the location and conditions at a level that respects privacy and safety.
- Records equipment, settings, placement and known limitations.
- Preserves original files and documents any processing.
- Describes controls, alternative explanations and unsuccessful tests.
- Reports uncertainty and avoids claims stronger than the evidence.
- Has permission for all identifiable recordings, quotations and images.

> **AERM Core Principle:** No anomaly is considered paranormal until environmental, psychological, technical, and scientific explanations have been systematically evaluated.
