# Case Study: A Motion Sensor Activates in an Unoccupied Room

### Case overview

This hypothetical training case concerns a passive infrared (PIR) motion sensor that activates several times in a room reported to be unoccupied. The scenario demonstrates a method; it does not claim that an actual AERM investigation produced these results.

### Understand the device

A PIR sensor detects changes in infrared radiation across zones in its field of view. It does not detect “movement” in the abstract. Sensor design, lens pattern, mounting height, temperature contrast, airflow and placement influence its response.

### Initial checks

The team records the make and model, sensitivity setting, detection range, power source and event-log behaviour. The room is mapped, including windows, heaters, vents, reflective surfaces and possible routes for animals or people.

### Competing hypotheses

Possible causes include a person entering unnoticed, an animal, warm or cool airflow, sunlight or a changing heat source, electrical instability, sensor placement, or a device fault. Each possibility requires a suitable test.

### Controlled method

The sensor is mounted according to manufacturer instructions. A camera records the field of view with consent and appropriate privacy controls. A second sensor is placed at a documented comparison location. The team performs a baseline test with known human movement at measured distances, then leaves the room under controlled conditions while maintaining safe observation.

### Analysis

Compare activation timestamps with video, environmental logs and building activity. Check whether the sensor triggers when HVAC changes or sunlight moves across the field. Repeat the session with one variable changed at a time. Do not infer that an empty room was truly unoccupied unless access and connected spaces were controlled.

### Reporting

State the sensor's detection principle and limitations. Distinguish “no person visible in the camera frame” from “no source was present.” Report missed detections and false triggers as well as activations.

### Conclusion

An activation is evidence that the sensor's output condition was met. It is not proof of an invisible agent. Understanding the detection mechanism is the first step toward interpreting the event responsibly.
