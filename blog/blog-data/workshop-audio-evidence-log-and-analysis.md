# Workshop: Create an Audio Evidence Log and Analyse a Recording Without Altering the Original

This workshop teaches a basic audio-evidence workflow. Participants will preserve an original recording, create a verified working copy, inspect its technical properties and produce a short analysis note. The exercise uses ordinary environmental audio and does not require participants to interpret ambiguous sounds as voices.

**Learning objectives.** Understand file integrity, sample rate, bit depth, waveform display, spectrograms and the difference between observation and interpretation.

**Materials.** A recorder or sample WAV file, computer, headphones, audio-analysis software, a spreadsheet or evidence-log template and a file-hashing utility. Use a recording made with consent and avoid private conversations.

**Step 1: preserve.** Copy the source file into a clearly named evidence folder. Do not trim or normalise it. Record its original file name, size, source, collection time and device. Calculate a SHA-256 hash.

**Step 2: create a working copy.** Duplicate the file and verify the copy's hash. Open only the working copy for analysis. Record the software name and version.

**Step 3: inspect.** Note duration, sample rate, channel count and peak level. View the waveform and spectrogram. Mark objective events such as a transient, clipping or a sustained tone. Do not label a sound as a word until independent listening supports that description.

**Step 4: listen independently.** Ask participants to listen without being told what they are expected to hear. Collect written descriptions before discussion. Compare the range of interpretations.

**Step 5: report.** Write a short note separating direct observations, processing steps and interpretations. Include the original hash and the working-copy hash. If enhancement is attempted, save it as a separate derivative and document every setting.

**Assessment.** A successful submission includes an intact source file, hash record, technical metadata, marked waveform or spectrogram, independent listening notes and a limitations paragraph.

**Conclusion.** Audio analysis is strongest when the original remains untouched and every transformation is visible. The workshop develops a repeatable habit that can be applied to any field recording.

## AERM Research and Reporting Checklist

Before publication, confirm that the report:
- States the question and the observation separately from any interpretation.
- Identifies the location and conditions at a level that respects privacy and safety.
- Records equipment, settings, placement and known limitations.
- Preserves original files and documents any processing.
- Describes controls, alternative explanations and unsuccessful tests.
- Reports uncertainty and avoids claims stronger than the evidence.
- Has permission for all identifiable recordings, quotations and images.
