# Interviewing a Witness Without Leading Them: A Practical Guide for Field Researchers

A witness interview can shape the evidence record before any instrument is switched on. Leading questions, group discussion and premature labels can introduce details that were not part of the original experience. A structured, neutral interview protects both the witness and the research.

**Prepare.** Explain the purpose, recording method, privacy choices and right to stop. Ask whether the person is comfortable being interviewed. Avoid promising that the team will prove a particular explanation.

**Begin with free recall.** Invite the person to describe what happened in their own words. Do not interrupt to correct sequence or suggest details. Record pauses and uncertainty respectfully.

**Use open prompts.** Ask “What happened next?” or “What did you notice about the sound?” rather than “Was it a woman's voice?” Follow up on time, distance, lighting, duration, direction and what the witness was doing.

**Separate observation and inference.** Ask what was directly seen or heard and what the person concluded afterward. A witness may say, “I heard three taps and thought someone was answering.” The taps are the reported sensory event; the answer is an interpretation.

**Avoid contamination.** Interview witnesses separately before they discuss accounts together, where practical. Do not play alleged recordings before the initial statement. If the witness later hears a recording, document that exposure and any change in their account.

**Close carefully.** Ask whether there is anything else they want to add. Explain next steps and how their information will be stored. Do not pressure them to revisit a distressing experience.

**AERM interview record.** Include consent, date/time, interviewer, location, recording reference, questions asked, interruptions, people present and any follow-up. Keep the original recording secure.

**Conclusion.** A good interview is not an interrogation. It is a respectful process for preserving what a person remembers while preventing the interviewer from supplying the story.

## AERM Research and Reporting Checklist

Before publication, confirm that the report:
- States the question and the observation separately from any interpretation.
- Identifies the location and conditions at a level that respects privacy and safety.
- Records equipment, settings, placement and known limitations.
- Preserves original files and documents any processing.
- Describes controls, alternative explanations and unsuccessful tests.
- Reports uncertainty and avoids claims stronger than the evidence.
- Has permission for all identifiable recordings, quotations and images.

> **AERM Core Principle:** No anomaly is considered paranormal until environmental, psychological, technical, and scientific explanations have been systematically evaluated.
