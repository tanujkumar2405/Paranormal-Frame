# Do Mirrors Really Attract or Trap Paranormal Activity? A Science-Based Examination

Mirrors are often assigned unusual powers in folklore: they are said to attract entities, trap spirits or reveal figures that cannot be seen directly. These claims are culturally interesting, but they should be separated from the physical behaviour of mirrors and the psychology of viewing reflections in uncertain conditions.

A plane mirror forms a virtual image by reflecting light. The apparent position of that image depends on geometry: the angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection. A mirror does not store a person's image or provide a known mechanism for attracting an invisible agent. Curved, aged, dirty or imperfect mirrors can distort images, while multiple reflective surfaces can create repeated visual patterns.

In dim light, the visual system has less reliable detail and colour information. A stationary face can appear unfamiliar when viewed continuously under poor illumination; small changes in gaze, shadow and adaptation may alter perceived features. Expectation can also influence what an observer reports. This does not mean every mirror-related experience is imaginary; it means the conditions should be tested.

**Myth versus fact.** Myth: mirrors independently generate figures. Fact: mirrors redirect available light and can produce complex reflections. Myth: a reflection that looks delayed proves an external presence. Fact: viewing angle, movement, frame rate, display latency and human visual processing can produce apparent timing differences. Myth: a mirror test is a reliable detection method. Fact: no validated mirror procedure establishes paranormal activity.

**A controlled test.** Photograph the room layout and light sources; mark observer and mirror positions; record illumination; use a fixed camera with known frame rate; repeat with the same positions; then alter one variable at a time, such as a lamp angle or a second reflective surface. Have observers describe what they see before discussing interpretations. Preserve the unedited footage.

**Interpretive limits.** A visual effect that cannot immediately be explained remains an unresolved observation, not proof of a paranormal cause. Mirrors may also be unsafe if damaged or used in unstable structures. Avoid candle-based experiments near old curtains, dust or combustible materials.

**Conclusion.** Mirrors are useful examples of how optics and perception interact. Investigators can respect cultural stories while testing physical claims with controlled lighting, geometry and repeatable recordings.

## AERM Research and Reporting Checklist

Before publication, confirm that the report:
- States the question and the observation separately from any interpretation.
- Identifies the location and conditions at a level that respects privacy and safety.
- Records equipment, settings, placement and known limitations.
- Preserves original files and documents any processing.
- Describes controls, alternative explanations and unsuccessful tests.
- Reports uncertainty and avoids claims stronger than the evidence.
- Has permission for all identifiable recordings, quotations and images.

> **AERM Core Principle:** No anomaly is considered paranormal until environmental, psychological, technical, and scientific explanations have been systematically evaluated.
