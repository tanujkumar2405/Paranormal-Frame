Field Safety in Paranormal Investigations: Risk Assessment and Professional Safety Guidelines

The greatest risk during an investigation is rarely the unexplained—it is the environment itself.

Field investigations often take place in unfamiliar environments, including abandoned buildings, forests, historical sites, industrial structures, and private properties. While these locations may contain reports of unusual activity, they frequently present genuine physical hazards that require careful planning and professional risk management.

At Anomalous Evidence Research & Media (AERM), investigator safety is considered a fundamental responsibility. Every investigation begins with a structured assessment of environmental risks before any evidence collection takes place.

No investigation is worth compromising personal safety.

---

Executive Summary

Field safety involves:

  • Risk assessment
  • Site planning
  • Emergency preparedness
  • Proper equipment
  • Team communication
  • Environmental awareness
  • Legal compliance
  • Ethical responsibility

A safe investigation is a professional investigation.

---

Why Field Safety Matters

Many investigation locations contain hazards unrelated to paranormal claims.

These may include:

  • Structural instability
  • Poor lighting
  • Uneven flooring
  • Sharp debris
  • Exposed electrical wiring
  • Wildlife
  • Hazardous weather
  • Chemical contamination

Ignoring these hazards places investigators at unnecessary risk and may compromise the investigation itself.

---

Conducting a Risk Assessment

Before entering any location, investigators should perform a preliminary risk assessment.

Questions to consider include:

  • Is the building structurally safe?
  • Are there signs of fire damage?
  • Is the site legally accessible?
  • Are hazardous materials present?
  • Is emergency access available?
  • What weather conditions are expected?
  • Is mobile communication available?

A documented risk assessment helps determine whether an investigation should proceed.

---

Never Investigate Alone

Solo investigations increase personal risk and reduce the ability to respond effectively during emergencies.

Whenever possible:

  • Work in teams.
  • Maintain visual or radio contact.
  • Establish meeting points.
  • Assign responsibilities.
  • Monitor team members throughout the investigation.

Teamwork improves both safety and investigative efficiency.

---

Essential Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)

Professional investigators should carry appropriate safety equipment.

Recommended PPE includes:

  • Safety boots with good grip
  • Protective gloves
  • Safety helmet (where required)
  • High-visibility vest (when appropriate)
  • Dust mask or respirator
  • Eye protection
  • Flashlight or headlamp
  • First aid kit

Protective equipment should match the environment being investigated.

---

Environmental Hazards

Investigators should remain aware of environmental risks such as:

Structural Hazards

  • Weak floors
  • Broken stairs
  • Unstable roofs
  • Loose walls
  • Falling debris

Electrical Hazards

  • Exposed wiring
  • Damaged electrical panels
  • Wet electrical equipment
  • Unknown power sources

Biological Hazards

  • Mold
  • Animal droppings
  • Insects
  • Snakes
  • Bats
  • Birds

Chemical Hazards

  • Fuel residues
  • Industrial chemicals
  • Asbestos
  • Cleaning agents
  • Contaminated water

Understanding these hazards is essential before entering any location.

---

Weather Awareness

Outdoor investigations require continuous monitoring of weather conditions.

Investigators should consider:

  • Heavy rainfall
  • High winds
  • Lightning
  • Extreme heat
  • Extreme cold
  • Flood risk

Weather conditions can change rapidly and significantly affect both safety and evidence collection.

---

Communication Planning

Every investigation should include a communication plan.

Teams should establish:

  • Emergency contacts
  • Communication methods
  • Check-in intervals
  • Evacuation procedures
  • Meeting locations

Reliable communication improves emergency response.

---

Emergency Preparedness

Every team member should know how to respond if an emergency occurs.

Preparation includes:

  • First aid knowledge
  • Emergency exit routes
  • Local emergency numbers
  • Hospital locations
  • Vehicle access
  • Backup lighting
  • Emergency supplies

Preparation reduces confusion during unexpected situations.

---

Equipment Safety

Investigation equipment should be inspected before every deployment.

Check:

  • Battery condition
  • Charging status
  • Cable integrity
  • Sensor operation
  • Camera function
  • Storage media

Faulty equipment can create unnecessary risks and affect evidence quality.

---

Respecting Legal Boundaries

Professional investigations should always comply with applicable laws.

Investigators should:

  • Obtain permission before entering private property.
  • Respect restricted areas.
  • Follow local regulations.
  • Avoid trespassing.
  • Preserve the condition of the location.

Responsible conduct protects both investigators and property owners.

---

Respecting the Investigation Site

Every location deserves professional treatment.

Investigators should:

  • Leave the site as it was found.
  • Avoid unnecessary disturbance.
  • Protect historical structures.
  • Minimize environmental impact.
  • Dispose of waste responsibly.

Good investigators protect the environments they study.

---

Personal Well-Being

Long investigations can be physically demanding.

Investigators should remember to:

  • Stay hydrated.
  • Eat regularly.
  • Dress appropriately.
  • Get adequate rest.
  • Monitor fatigue.
  • Recognize signs of heat exhaustion or dehydration.

Good physical condition supports better decision-making.

---

Common Safety Mistakes

Avoid these common errors:

  • Entering unsafe structures.
  • Ignoring weather warnings.
  • Investigating alone.
  • Carrying inadequate lighting.
  • Wearing unsuitable footwear.
  • Ignoring emergency planning.
  • Taking unnecessary risks for photographs or videos.
  • Allowing curiosity to override safety.

Professional judgment should always outweigh curiosity.

---

AERM's Safety Philosophy

Safety is integrated into every stage of an investigation.

AERM encourages investigators to:

  • Plan carefully.
  • Assess risks objectively.
  • Protect themselves and others.
  • Respect property and communities.
  • Suspend investigations whenever conditions become unsafe.

No observation or piece of evidence justifies unnecessary danger.

---

Conclusion

Field safety is not an optional part of investigation—it is the foundation upon which every successful investigation is built.

Professional investigators understand that responsible research begins with protecting people, respecting locations, and managing risks through preparation and disciplined decision-making.

At AERM, we believe that careful planning, environmental awareness, and teamwork allow investigators to conduct meaningful research while maintaining the highest standards of safety and professionalism.

Evidence can always be collected another day. Your safety cannot be replaced.

---

Field Safety Checklist

Before every investigation, confirm that:

  • □ Permission has been obtained.
  • □ Risk assessment is complete.
  • □ Weather has been checked.
  • □ Emergency contacts are available.
  • □ Communication devices are working.
  • □ First aid kit is packed.
  • □ Batteries are fully charged.
  • □ Personal protective equipment is available.
  • □ Investigation team has been briefed.
  • □ Emergency exit routes are identified.
