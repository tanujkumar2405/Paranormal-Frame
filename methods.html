Methods & Investigative Approach

How Paranormal Frame Documents Unexplained Phenomena

Paranormal investigation at Paranormal Frame is an observational study focused on documentation, systematic analysis, and evidence preservation—not assumption or predetermined conclusion. Our methodology emphasizes neutrality, ethical conduct, and transparent reporting of findings from India's most historically significant locations, implemented via PFIRS (Paranormal Forensic Investigation Reporting System) to ensure consistent, evidence-based investigations.

The Foundation of Responsible Investigation

Why methodology matters in paranormal research

Paranormal investigations require systematic methodology to distinguish genuine anomalies from misidentification, equipment artifacts, environmental factors, or observer bias. At Paranormal Frame, we approach each investigation with structured protocols designed to:

Maintain Neutrality

Document observations without assumption or predetermined conclusion. Allow data to speak for itself rather than forcing findings into predetermined narratives.

Preserve Evidence

Collect data without manipulation, enhancement, or editing. Raw recordings and unaltered instrumental readings form the basis of all analysis.

Ensure Ethics

Conduct investigations with permission, respect cultural and religious significance, prioritize team safety, and protect participant privacy.

Enable Verification

Document methodology thoroughly so findings can be independently reviewed and alternative explanations can be systematically evaluated.

Six Phases of Investigation

How paranormal investigations are structured and conducted

1

Case Assessment & Background Review

Before any investigation begins, we thoroughly review submitted reports and location information:

  • Study submitted documentation and observer accounts
  • Research location history, architecture, and construction records
  • Identify environmental factors that may explain reported phenomena
  • Evaluate whether systematic investigation is appropriate and feasible
  • Document preliminary hypotheses for testing during investigation

Purpose: Establish investigation scope and prepare testing framework.

2

Site Survey & Environmental Baseline

Initial site survey establishes baseline environmental conditions before monitoring begins:

  • Daytime walkthrough to identify location layout, access points, and potential hazards
  • Map electrical wiring, power sources, wireless transmitters, and metal structures
  • Establish baseline readings for electromagnetic fields, ambient temperature, and background sound
  • Document construction materials and architectural features
  • Identify potential sources of environmental variation (HVAC, traffic, weather exposure)

Purpose: Create reference data against which investigation readings are compared.

3

Controlled Observation Period

Extended monitoring under controlled conditions to establish baseline activity patterns:

  • Quiet observation periods with minimal team movement or interference
  • Continuous instrument deployment recording environmental parameters
  • Fixed recording positions to minimize variable factors
  • Time-stamped documentation of all instrumental readings and observations
  • Team remains alert but non-interactive to preserve baseline conditions

Purpose: Identify normal activity patterns and environmental variations.

4

Active Documentation Sessions

Structured investigation sessions using prepared observation protocols:

  • Audio recording sessions capturing all environmental sounds and vocalizations
  • Continuous environmental monitoring with instruments deployed in fixed positions
  • Visual documentation through low-light and thermal imaging
  • Motion tracking and physical environment observation
  • Multiple independent observers recording separate documentation
  • All activity time-stamped for correlation analysis

Purpose: Collect comprehensive multi-instrument data during investigation period.

5

Evidence Review & Analysis

Systematic examination of collected data to identify patterns and anomalies:

  • Review all raw recordings without manipulation or enhancement
  • Identify anomalous readings and correlate with observer accounts
  • Cross-reference instrumental data with environmental factors
  • Systematically rule out known causes and expected sources
  • Evaluate whether patterns remain unexplained after analysis
  • Document all analysis processes for verification and transparency

Purpose: Objectively evaluate findings against baseline and environmental factors.

6

Conclusion & Archiving

Final classification of findings and secure documentation archiving:

  • Observed Anomaly: Documented phenomenon that remains unexplained after systematic analysis
  • Inconclusive: Insufficient data or ambiguous findings requiring additional investigation
  • No Significant Activity: Findings explained by environmental or instrumental factors
  • Archive all materials with complete metadata and timestamps
  • Publish findings responsibly through documentary presentation
  • Make findings available for peer review and independent analysis

Purpose: Provide transparent, verifiable conclusions and maintain complete research record.

Core Investigative Approaches

Methods employed depending on case characteristics and location

Observational Approach

Passive documentation of location activity without interaction. Team remains present but non-interactive to minimize environmental interference. Observers record all activity, sounds, and phenomena over extended periods.

Environmental Approach

Intensive monitoring of environmental variables over time to identify patterns, fluctuations, or anomalous readings. Establishes whether environmental factors correlate with reported phenomena.

Comparative Analysis

Direct comparison of baseline conditions (pre-investigation) versus investigation readings. Anomalies are identified as significant variations from established baseline rather than arbitrary thresholds.

Skeptical Review

Active systematic attempt to rule out known causes and explain findings through environmental factors. We prioritize non-paranormal explanations and eliminate them methodically before considering alternative hypotheses.

Historical Context Analysis

Understanding location history, construction, past events, and cultural significance without assuming causation. Historical context informs investigation design but does not influence interpretation of findings.

PFIRS — Paranormal Forensic Investigation Reporting System

AERM's standardized case documentation & investigation framework

PFIRS (Paranormal Forensic Investigation Reporting System) is AERM's standardized case documentation and investigation framework. It ensures every investigation follows scientific methodology, objective observation, and consistent evidence collection rather than assumptions or personal beliefs.

Objectives

  • Standardize paranormal investigation procedures.
  • Maintain accurate and transparent case records.
  • Eliminate investigator bias through evidence-based documentation.
  • Rule out environmental, psychological, technical, and natural explanations before considering unexplained phenomena.
  • Preserve evidence with proper chain-of-custody procedures.
  • Produce professional investigation reports suitable for research and education.

Core Principles

  • Evidence before assumptions.
  • Every claim requires documentation.
  • Natural explanations must always be investigated first.
  • Multiple sources of evidence are preferred over a single observation.
  • Investigations should be repeatable whenever possible.
  • Transparency and integrity are essential throughout the investigation.

Investigation Workflow

  4. Background Research
  5. Site Survey
  6. Environmental Baseline Testing
  7. Witness Interviews
  8. Evidence Collection
  9. Environmental and Technical Analysis
  10. Cross-Verification
  11. Report Preparation
  12. Final Classification

Types of Evidence Collected

Environmental and instrumental data collected under PFIRS includes:

  • Environmental measurements (temperature, humidity, EMF, air quality)
  • Audio recordings
  • Video recordings
  • Photographs
  • Thermal imagery
  • Soil and water samples (when applicable)
  • Air quality and chemical observations
  • Biological indicators
  • Witness statements
  • Historical records
  • Physical trace evidence

Scientific Rule-Out Process

PFIRS emphasizes identifying conventional explanations before labeling any event as unexplained. Investigators systematically examine:

  • Electrical interference and faulty wiring
  • EMF sources and RF noise
  • Structural movement and settling
  • Weather conditions and drafts
  • Wildlife activity and insects
  • Chemical contamination and air quality
  • Mold, fungal growth, and biological agents
  • Carbon monoxide and other toxic exposures
  • Infrasound and vibration
  • Optical illusions and lens artifacts
  • Psychological factors and perceptual bias
  • Equipment malfunction

Case Classification

After full analysis, PFIRS classifies cases into categories such as:

  • Explained
  • Insufficient Evidence
  • Inconclusive
  • Under Investigation
  • Unexplained (only when supported by documented evidence and after reasonable conventional explanations have been ruled out)

Documentation Standards

Each PFIRS report includes standardized metadata and evidence fields to ensure consistency and chain-of-custody:

  • Case ID
  • Investigation date and location
  • Investigation team
  • Weather conditions
  • Equipment list
  • Witness information
  • Timeline of events
  • Environmental readings
  • Evidence inventory
  • Analysis summary
  • Conclusion and recommendations
  • Investigator signatures

Benefits of PFIRS

  • Professional and consistent investigations
  • Improved evidence quality
  • Easier peer review and research reuse
  • Reduced investigator bias
  • Increased public credibility and transparency
  • Long-term searchable case archive

Ethical & Safety Standards

Core principles guiding all investigations

Permission & Access

All investigations require explicit written permission from location owners or administrators. No unauthorized access, trespassing, or entry to restricted areas. Research conducted only at times and locations explicitly agreed upon.

Cultural & Religious Respect

Investigations at heritage sites and culturally significant locations conducted with deep respect for religious beliefs and cultural practices. Research does not violate sacred spaces, interfere with worship, or disrespect community traditions.

No Provocation or Rituals

Investigations do not include séances, rituals, provocation, or attempts to communicate with reported entities. We observe and document; we do not attempt to initiate contact or encourage phenomena.

Team Safety Protocols

All investigations include comprehensive safety planning, hazard identification, first aid resources, and communication systems. Team members are trained in emergency procedures. Investigations are terminated if unsafe conditions develop.

Data Privacy & Consent

Observer names and personal information are protected unless explicitly consented for publication. Witness accounts are documented confidentially. Data is stored securely and shared only with research participants and authorized reviewers.

What We Do NOT Do

Practices that compromise research integrity

To maintain credibility and research integrity, Paranormal Frame explicitly does not:

  • Claim proof of paranormal phenomena. Anomalies are documented and analyzed; they are not presented as definitive evidence of supernatural activity.
  • Engage in spirit communication or channeling. We do not attempt to communicate with, summon, or interact with reported entities.
  • Conduct rituals or exorcisms. Our role is documentation and analysis, not spiritual intervention.
  • Manipulate, enhance, or edit raw evidence. All data presented is unaltered and raw from source instruments.
  • Stage events or create false phenomena. Investigations are genuine research, not entertainment productions.
  • Use sensational language or misleading claims. Reporting is objective and evidence-based without exaggeration.
  • Create misleading thumbnails or clickbait. Visual presentation is honest and accurately represents content.
  • Proceed without informed consent. Subjects and locations must agree to documentation and any public release.

Methods & Tools Integration

How investigative approaches are supported by technology

The methodology outlined above is enabled by carefully selected investigative tools. Each tool serves a specific function within the broader research framework-none proves paranormal activity, but together they provide comprehensive environmental documentation and anomaly detection.

Audio recorders document environmental sounds during observation periods. EMF meters establish electromagnetic baselines and detect field variations. Temperature sensors identify thermal anomalies. Visual documentation captures low-light and infrared phenomena. Motion sensors detect physical disturbances. Environmental logging correlates all data with observer accounts.

For detailed explanation of how each tool works, its capabilities, and its limitations, see our Investigation Tools & Field Methods section. Understanding tool limitations is as important as understanding their capabilities.

Paranormal Investigation Methods - FAQs

Questions about our research protocols and investigation approach

Multiple phases allow us to establish baseline environmental conditions, document variations across different times/dates, and distinguish between anomalies and normal environmental fluctuations. Baseline measurements are critical for identifying genuine anomalies and distinguishing paranormal signals from background noise or equipment artifacts.

We employ multiple independent observers, double-blind analysis where possible, alternative hypothesis evaluation, and peer review of findings. We actively seek non-paranormal explanations before accepting anomalies as potentially paranormal. Equipment calibration and standardized protocols reduce subjective interpretation.

EVP (Electronic Voice Phenomena) refers to unaccountable voices or sounds recorded during investigations. We use digital audio recorders and analyze recordings for anomalies not attributable to environmental sources, interference, or pareidolia (pattern recognition artifacts). Analysis includes spectrographic examination, frequency analysis, and peer evaluation before classification.

We employ synchronized recording systems that timestamp all measurements simultaneously. Data from EMF meters, thermal cameras, audio recorders, and motion sensors are analyzed for temporal correlation. Simultaneous anomalies across multiple instruments provide stronger evidence than isolated readings from single devices.

Our ethical standards include: respecting privacy and property rights, obtaining proper permissions from locations, protecting participant identity, avoiding exploitation of sensitive beliefs, declining sensationalism, and maintaining transparency about methodology and findings. We prioritize cultural respect at heritage and spiritually significant sites.

Transparent reporting is fundamental to our methodology. We publish inconclusive and refutable findings with full documentation of investigation conditions, analysis performed, and alternative explanations identified. Negative results are as valuable scientifically as positive findings in advancing paranormal research methodology.
